# Lore bible: the truth of the Well

The canon of *Le Puits des Âmes / The Well of Souls*. Every name, line of lore, dialogue, fragment
and ending must agree with this page. The **voice** (vocabulary, naming patterns, who speaks how)
is in `docs/lexicon.md`; this page is **what is true**. To write lore, use the `lore` skill
(`.claude/skills/lore/`).

French is written first; English is its equal, not a word-for-word copy. Names below are given
FR / EN.

## 1. The one-paragraph truth (never shown whole to the player)

Under Kagari lies the Gueule (the Maw), an old hunger at the bottom of the Well that gives
plenty in exchange for souls. Centuries ago the kingdom of **Solhar** worshipped it as a sun and
fed it its people until it swallowed the kingdom whole. A nameless knight sealed it and gave
his name to the seal; Kagari was built on top, with a shrine, a bell and a lantern that must
never go out. On a moonless night, **Maître Tôma**, keeper of the shrine and the hero's master,
put out every flame of Kagari and fed the village's souls to the Maw, to buy back his drowned
daughter **Hana**. He spared one soul: the disciple holding the lantern. The hero goes down to
bring the souls back, and at the bottom finds his master, worn by the Maw like a mask.

The player learns this **in pieces, over many runs**. No single line states it.

## 2. Timeline

| When | What happened |
|---|---|
| Ages ago | Solhar, a kingdom under a sky that is now stone, worships a "sun" that answers prayers. The sun is the Maw's mask. The Dîme (Tithe): souls given at blood altars for harvests, gold, victories. |
| The fall of Solhar | The king, greedy for more, gives the whole tithe at once, then his people. When no soul is left, the Maw takes the kingdom. Solhar becomes the **Kingdom of Dust**; the king becomes the **Gilded Hoarder** on his hoard; the false god becomes the **Dead Sun**, buried with them. |
| The sealing | A knight of Solhar (or of no land: the texts disagree on purpose) goes down and seals the Maw with a rite that costs his name. He becomes **the Nameless Effigy**, "He Who Does Not Rest", guarding the seal in the Nameless Charnel. Brann forged his blades. |
| The founding of Kagari | Settlers build Kagari over the Well: a shrine, a bronze **bell**, and the **lantern** whose flame keeps the Well asleep. Keepers succeed each other; their statues stand down the Well (the Keeper's Statues, « Statues du Gardien »). The village lays its dead in the **Ossuary**, which later sinks into the Well as it stirs. Below it, unknown to Kagari, lie the ruins of Solhar: its drowned archives, its **Pyre Temple**, its buried city. |
| Some years ago | Hana, Tôma's daughter, drowns in the spring under the village (the **Spring of the Drowned**). Tôma, keeper of the shrine, raises an orphan as his disciple: the hero. |
| The years after | Tôma searches the drowned archives of Solhar (the **Hushed Archive** (les Archives Muettes)) and finds the old pact: the Maw gives back what it took, if it is fed. |
| The moonless night | Tôma puts out every flame of Kagari, unbinds the seal's last knot and offers the village's souls. The villagers sleep and do not wake. He leaves one lantern burning, in the disciple's hand: he could not give him too. He goes down. |
| The game | The hero descends. The souls brought back wake the shrine (the Tree of Souls). The **Veilleuse**, who never slept, waits at the shrine. |

## 3. The antagonist: Maître Tôma, the Oathbreaker (le Gardien parjure)

- **Who:** the keeper of Kagari's shrine, master of the hero, father of Hana. Patient, exact,
  kind to the hero; a man who believed rules keep the dark away until the dark took his child.
- **Want:** Hana back. Not power, not revenge. He tells himself the village will wake when Hana
  does; he is lying to himself and half knows it.
- **Act:** fed Kagari to the Maw, bound the dead of the Well as wardens to guard the souls until
  the feast, went down to the bottom to wait for the Maw to keep its word.
- **Truth he refuses:** the Maw never gives back. What waits with him is not Hana but a thing
  wearing her voice (heard at the Spring of the Drowned).
- **How he shows before the end:** never in person. His **journal pages** (fragments), the
  wardens' lines ("the keeper chained me here"), the Veilleuse's silences, the shrine's
  extinguished lamps, a sword cut in the Effigy's seal made with the hero's own school.
- **The final fight** (built: docs/design/monsters.md, "The bottom of the Well"): phase 1, Tôma
  fights with the hero's own techniques (a dash strike, a long-drawn wide cut, the lantern's
  light turned against him: a violet flame closing on the ground under the hero); phase 2,
  when he falls to his knees, **the Maw** (la Gueule) rises behind him out of a pool of tar, takes
  his kneeling body into its mouth and wears him like a mask. It can only be hurt when its jaw
  opens: then Tôma is seen inside, standing. When the Maw dies its golden mask crumbles and
  leaves him standing in the dark, freed.
- **His words at the bottom:** waking, « Tu es venu, mon élève. » / "You came, my pupil."; on
  his knees, « Ta garde est juste, mon élève. » / "Your guard is true, my pupil." (a teacher's
  last judgement: he is proud of the cut that beat him).
- **Voice:** few words, a teacher's calm, never shouts. Calls the hero "mon élève" / "my pupil",
  never by a name (the hero has none).

### 6b. What the voices hold (the dialogue: `data/story/dialogue/`)

Facts the Vigil-Keeper's and the keepers' lines hold; anything new written for them goes here.

- **The Vigil-Keeper** has no eyes left, she **hears**: the bell tells her who took the hero (« le
  nom que la cloche m'a soufflé »), she hears a lantern burn (the hero's crackles like a child's
  laugh), an arrow whistle, souls climbing the bell rope "warm as bread"; she smells ash. She
  never sleeps; her bell rings only for souls coming home. While the hero's lantern burns at the
  shrine, the Well gives him back to her (the death screen). She quotes her lessons as "ton
  maître disait..." (watch the shoulder, not the blade; a guard is knowing when not to strike)
  and believes, until the reveal, that the master went down before the hero to bring the souls
  back. Her masters spoke of a kingdom under Kagari that prayed to a hungry sun (a tale for
  children, she thought). After the first victory she admits the lamps were blown out **by hand,
  in the order of the rite**, and will not hear who knows that order; after the Effigy she
  refuses that "a blade of our school" means anyone ("many learned the guard here"). Once the
  keeper is named she breaks: she rocked Hana beside Tôma; he gave the village to a hunger that
  gives nothing back; he spared the hero because he could not give him too, "all I still love of
  him". After "ring the bell" she sleeps for the first time in many nights; after "take his
  place" Tôma knocks at every door of the village to answer for it, and she keeps the hero's
  lantern as she kept his.
- **The shrine's lamps**: seven lamps in niches over the Well, put out by hand on the moonless
  night; one burns again for every share of souls offered to the Tree (the title screen).
- **The Old Wanderer** sold his name to the Well for the right to trade forever (the Well lets
  him through: a man without a name does not feed it); every coin bears the same king's face; he
  sold spices in Solhar "when its sky was not stone" and the king paid in gold, never in bread;
  **Ostarion owes him three sacks of saffron**; the Sandchoked City was his finest market (a stall
  with blue shutters is his); after the reveal: **Tôma once bought a lantern without a wick from
  him** (the unlit lantern he cut the seal with). He hints at the Hoarder's deed (buy nothing).
- **Brann** served an army "under a sky that burned" (Solhar); metal there tasted of salt and
  gold; he knows the steel of the Azure Wyrm's frost-proof chains and the hand that hammered it
  (his); he forged the Effigy's blades weeping into the quench; a seal is "a lock of iron and of
  name": the first keeper gave the name, Brann the iron; after the reveal he tells of Tôma
  ordering the chains, given without looking at him.
- **The Grinning Spirit** bet everything on a double six one night in Solhar; the Well rolled a
  seven on two dice. Solhar played dice on the temple stairs: the king bet his people, the sun
  took all the shares. After the reveal: the master bet the whole village on one throw, believing
  the Well gives back what is bet.

## 7. What the player learns, and when (revelation schedule)

Lines and fragments are gated by **progress counters** (runs, deaths, wardens slain, acts reached,
fragments found, the Effigy slain, victories). A rough order:

1. **First runs:** the Well woke, Kagari sleeps, the hero alone was spared. The Veilleuse is there.
2. **Act II reached:** the dead were laid down by Kagari; the Spring of the Drowned; a girl's voice.
3. **Act III reached / first fragments:** Solhar existed; the pyres fed a "sun"; someone read the
   archives recently (fresh candle wax, a keeper's seal).
4. **First victory:** the souls come back, the bell rings, "the Well goes deeper still" (the

5. **The Nameless Effigy slain:** the seal was cut, by a blade of the hero's own school.

## 8. Rules of the canon

- The Maw (la Gueule) is the Well's hunger. It never speaks as "I"; things speak for it (the Dead
  Sun, the voice in the spring).
- Souls are not destroyed until eaten: the hero can bring them back. That is why the shrine grows
  strong (the Tree of Souls is the village's souls lending their strength).
- The lantern is the hero's life-line: the Threshold, the Dawn Breach and the light of the shrine
  are all the lantern. If it goes out, the run ends ("La lanterne s'éteint... pour cette fois.").
- Gold is Solhar's: everything gold in the Well (coins, the Hoarder, the gilded moths) comes
  from the fallen kingdom.
- Demons are not from elsewhere: the Damned are what the Maw leaves of the souls it ate.
- Kagari is Japanese-rooted (shrine, bell, ninja, rônin, tengu, kappa); Solhar is older and
  foreign (sun cult, desert, gold, plate armor). Names follow each: Tôma, Hana, Kagari; Solhar,
  Brann.
- Time in the Well is wrong: the dead remember centuries as nights.
- The hero has no name, never speaks in lines (the player is "tu" / "you").

## Kagari between descents

The village square is playable. The Well stands at its centre; the Vigil-Keeper keeps the
shrine beside it. Black roots follow the cracks toward the sleeping houses. This first
chapter has a fixed infestation front, pushed back by the player's actions, never an
offline timer or a punishment for dying.

Sayo, who once taught the mourners, and Ren, a village lantern-mender, still sleep. What
stands at their doors is an echo caught in the lantern's light, not a third survivor of
the moonless night. Their faint words reveal daily life, never the culprit. Recovering
Ren's ember-box from the second floor and returning it to the Vigil-Keeper lets his
soul find its door: he wakes, the eastern roots wither and a window lights. Other houses
remain asleep. This small rescue does not resolve the village's fate or replace the endings.

Once Ren's lamp burns again, Sayo's echo can remember what is missing: the written last verse
of the mourning song she taught at Kagari's doors. Its sheet lies on the fourth floor, carried
down with the village's leavings. Returning it to her lets her finish the song and wake; the
western roots wither and her window lights. She remembers the mourners, not who put out the
lamps. The remaining sleepers still wait. This is another local rescue, never the ending's
awakening of all Kagari.

Once Sayo sings again she remembers who grew the flowers she sang over: old **Gen**, the
gardener of the cottage on the southern lane. He grew Kagari's vegetables and the white flowers
it lays on its graves, and every autumn he carried a bundle down to the Ossuary's first niches.
When the Well stirred and the Ossuary sank, his seed pouch went down with them; it lies on the
fifth floor, where the stone smells of wet earth. His echo frets about the frost and the rows he
has not sown. Returning the pouch wakes him: the roots let go of his cottage, its door opens, and
his white rows flower again. He remembers his hens and his frost, not the moonless night. The
roots that crept from the Well toward each sleeping home through the grass wither one trail at
a time, as each sleeper wakes; the square around the Well clears, the rest of Kagari sleeps.

Their conversations stay close to daily life. Ren returns to his wicks because familiar work
steadies his hands; Sayo leaves her door open for the next neighbour to return. The Veilleuse
hears the square becoming inhabited again, and the Wanderer complains fondly about customers
finding fault with his stock. These are signs of partial recovery, never proof that all Kagari
is saved. Optional questions and quest choices express the player's intent; the hero remains
unvoiced. Sayo's teal hair and clothing palette distinguish her from the Veilleuse's silver and
bell-rope red while reusing the existing figure and portrait art.

The Well took Kagari's people, not its beasts. The hens still scratch in the vegetable rows of a
cottage whose gardener sleeps, the market cat keeps the Wanderer's stall, and the woodcutter's
dog waits at a door that does not open. They are only animals: they neither speak nor know
anything of the moonless night, and their patience is the village's, not a sign.

The Old Wanderer can cross the Well's rim as he crosses its floors. At the surface he
barters work for souls that are released into Kagari's lamps, never eaten or imprisoned:
he sells the lighting of the market and the memorial court, once each. These services
change the village, not the starting kit or the fairness of the daily descent.

Each hero who chooses to hold the seal is remembered in stone, with the arms he carried.
The court shows the latest memorial; its register recalls every recorded predecessor.
The Well's repeating night does not erase these stones. A release frees the Keeper below
but leaves his memorial above. Existing saves can remember the current Keeper; missing
old full appearances cannot be invented from the codex's abbreviated freed records.

Kagari is an inhabited village whose souls were drawn into the Well; its houses and temple
are not the ruins of Solhar. Reused exterior art is temporary until the village asset pass.
Its daylight returns independently of the Well's repeating night below. Rescued neighbours
resume work, visits and ordinary sleep; unrecovered echoes remain bound to their doors. The
Veilleuse keeps watch at the shrine and Well through the night. Ordinary night rest must never
be confused with the soul-loss that the rescue quests resolve.

The Temple of the Last Flame stands north of the square. Its inhabited shrine remains a
place of care: the Veilleuse tends it morning and evening; Sayo comes to pray once restored.
It is not another dungeon or a temple of Solhar. No new survivor or revelation is added by
entering the temple.

A sleeping house looks as its sleeper does: moss and the Well's roots over it, its door shut to
the living. When its sleeper wakes the moss is gone with the roots and the door opens. Sayo's
house is a room of songs: her shelves, the low table where she writes, the paper lantern Ren made
her. Ren's workshop smells of oil; lanterns wait on his bench for their wicks. Entering a home
reveals nothing of the moonless night; it shows what the Well took and gave back: ordinary life.
